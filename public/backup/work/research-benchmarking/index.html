Work/Benchmarking

Benchmarking digital product quality over time.

A single usability study tells you what is happening now. A benchmarking program tells you whether things are getting better or worse. Tracking effectiveness, efficiency, satisfaction, and desirability at regular intervals gives product and engineering teams evidence of whether changes are actually improving the experience — or just shipping features.

Built from scratch at RAA and run across multiple product areas as a continuous measurement practice.

Scope

Insurance, travel, membership, identity

Role

Senior UX Design Lead

Tools

UserTesting.com, Optimal Workshop, custom metrics framework

The Framework

Four dimensions. Tracked consistently. Compared over time.

The benchmarking framework measures four dimensions across each product area. Effectiveness — can users complete the task at all? Efficiency — how long does it take and how many errors occur? Satisfaction — how do users rate the experience? Desirability — does the experience reflect the brand qualities the business wants to be associated with?

Each study is run to a standardised protocol so results can be compared across time periods. A change in score means something because the methodology is consistent. When a metric improves after a delivery, there is evidence the change worked. When it does not improve, there is a signal that something else needs investigating. The data feeds directly into the journey map, keeping prioritisation grounded in measurement rather than assumption.

Research Operations

Built from scratch — repository, protocols, and practice.

Before the research operations function existed at RAA, findings were produced for individual projects and then essentially lost. No shared repository. No way to build on previous work. Each new project started from scratch.

Research operations introduced a structured repository tagging findings to specific products and stages of the experience, a standardised testing process so findings could be compared across studies over time, and a regular benchmarking cadence that gave product and engineering teams a consistent signal about experience quality. The infrastructure changed how the organisation made decisions — from opinion-led to evidence-led.

Connected to Journey Maps

Measurement that feeds back into prioritisation.

Benchmarking results feed directly into the journey map at the stage of the experience where each study ran. When a metric shifts — a drop in completion rate, a rise in error frequency, a fall in satisfaction score — the map shows exactly which stage is affected and what the current prioritised responses are.

That connection between measurement and action is what makes the system useful. Without it, benchmarking produces reports that get filed. With it, benchmarking shapes what the team works on next.
