Research Operations

Built a formal research practice at RAA from nothing.

When I joined RAA, design decisions were made on stakeholder opinion, past convention, or whoever spoke most confidently in the room. There was no research repository, no shared testing methodology, no way to track whether the experience was improving over time.

Over five years, I built Research Operations from the ground up, including a research repository, team onboarding documentation, a UX metrics framework, and the testing cadences that made continuous validation part of how the team worked rather than an event scheduled before launch.

Tools: UserTesting.com, Optimal Workshop, Figma prototypes, analytics synthesis

Scope: Moderated and unmoderated usability testing, benchmarking studies, confidence testing, research synthesis

The Benchmarking Dashboard

The dashboard above shows quantitative research data for the RAA Insurance home and contents quote flow. Effectiveness measures whether users completed the task successfully (39 success, 1 fail). Efficiency shows time-on-task distribution with a mean of 4 minutes. Satisfaction and usefulness are measured via post-task Likert scales. Desirability captures the emotional quality of the experience through word choice.

This format makes the research actionable and comparable over time. When a feature ships, the next benchmarking study either confirms the improvement or surfaces new issues, giving the team a data-backed case for what to prioritise next.

User Research

Quantitative benchmarking is only half the picture. Moderated usability sessions, ethnographic interviews, and field research provide the qualitative layer: the reasons behind the numbers, the language users actually use, the mental models that the interface does or does not match.

Research findings were synthesised into personas, journey maps, and insight repositories that the whole team could reference, turning individual research sessions into organisational knowledge rather than notes on someone's hard drive.
