Work/AI Tooling

AI in the design and product process.

AI tools are most useful when they reduce the overhead that competes with the work that requires judgment. Synthesis, documentation, spec generation, pattern identification — when AI handles these, the time that is freed up goes toward discovery, testing, and the decisions that actually move product quality forward.

Faster discovery means the business can validate or redirect product decisions faster. That is a business outcome argument, not a craft efficiency argument.

Scope

Research, design, delivery, knowledge management

Role

UX Manager, Senior UX Design Lead

Tools

Claude, Figma AI, journey management AI, UserTesting

The Principle

Faster discovery means better product decisions, sooner.

When AI compresses the time it takes to synthesise research, generate journey map structures from raw notes, or produce component documentation from a design file, the gain is not just hours saved. It is earlier access to the insight that would have taken a week to surface manually — which means product decisions get better information sooner, and the cost of being wrong about a direction is lower.

This framing shapes how AI has been used across the team's process. Not as a way to do design faster at the expense of quality, but as a way to reduce the administrative overhead that competes with quality.

Research Synthesis

From raw sessions to actionable insights faster.

Research synthesis is one of the most time-consuming parts of discovery — reading session transcripts, tagging observations, identifying patterns across participants, and turning raw notes into a coherent problem statement. AI tools now assist with all of this.

The practical outcome: a research round that previously took several days to synthesise can be turned around in hours. The designer spends their time reviewing and refining AI-generated patterns rather than building them from scratch. The quality of the insight is the same. The time to action is significantly shorter.

Rapid Prototyping

Testing ideas before committing to build.

AI-assisted prototyping tools can scan a live product and generate a working replica with a new feature integrated — so users test in context rather than against an abstract mockup. The result is more accurate feedback about whether the design works in the real environment.

Figma's AI tools assist with variant generation, accessibility checking, and spec annotation — reducing the documentation overhead that would otherwise compete with design and testing time.

Orchestration Layer

Institutional knowledge made accessible across a distributed team.

As the Experience Design team reduced in size, institutional knowledge became a risk. Individual designers knew things that were not documented. When someone left, that knowledge went with them.

The orchestration layer addressed this by connecting the design system documentation, journey maps, research repository, live products, and internal standards — WCAG guidelines, solution architecture, brand requirements, legal constraints — into a single queryable source via a Slackbot. A designer in a product squad could ask a question in the channel they were already working in and get a synthesised answer from all available sources rather than searching across four tools manually.

The outcome: a smaller team maintaining the same delivery quality and knowledge access because the institutional knowledge was finally accessible rather than locked in individual heads or buried in folders.
