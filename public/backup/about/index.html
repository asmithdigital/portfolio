About

Senior UX designer and team manager based in Adelaide.

Twenty years across product design, front-end development, and UX team management. Interested in how teams make design decisions and how to build the practice around them.

Background

Started as a self-taught web designer and front-end developer, building websites for clients across agency, media, and corporate environments. Moved into UX at the University of Adelaide, where I led UX practice and introduced a design system approach across the university's digital products.

Seven years at RAA after that, first as the senior UX designer across travel, insurance, member portal, payments, and renewals, then as UX Manager responsible for the team, the practice, and delivery quality. Two decades of real products, real users, and real engineering constraints.

How I Work

Research before design. I work within Agile product squads alongside product managers, engineers, analytics teams, and business stakeholders — reviewing what the data is showing, attending roadmap and vision discussions, facilitating discovery workshops, and bringing customer and usability evidence into the decisions the team is making. The design work is grounded in that context, not separate from it.

Design system thinking from the start. Consistency comes from captured decisions, documented components, and governance that maintains them. When the system is working, product and engineering teams can move faster and the design team spends more time on the problems that actually need solving.

Close to delivery and to product. I write clear specs, work alongside engineers as a peer — starting in code means feasibility conversations are grounded in shared understanding — and stay involved through build, QA, and the measurement that follows.

Currently studying product management

Product management is the discipline responsible for deciding what gets built, for whom, and why — balancing customer needs, business viability, and technical feasibility. A product manager works as part of a trio with design and engineering, owning the problem space so the team can focus on solving the right thing rather than building the wrong thing well.

I am studying it because I have been working inside that trio for over a decade — contributing the design and customer insight seat alongside product managers and engineers. Understanding the formal discipline more deeply makes that partnership more effective and opens the path to taking on product responsibilities directly.
